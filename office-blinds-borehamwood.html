The local picture

The offices we see in Borehamwood

Borehamwood is a smaller town with a distinctive mix: ordinary business space alongside offices that serve the film and television industry. These are the kinds of premises worth describing and the window problems they tend to bring.

Offices around Elstree Way and the business parks

Purpose-built office blocks and business parks give runs of similar windows, often with one elevation facing the sun for most of the day. The job is usually consistent glare and heat control across a full run.

Production and post-production offices

Borehamwood is closely associated with film and television, with sites such as Elstree Studios and the BBC Elstree Centre in the area. Offices that serve that industry often include edit suites and viewing rooms where screens need glare control and some rooms need real darkness. This describes the type of premises in the town, not work done for any named company.

Small serviced offices

Small suites and shared workspaces tend to have a handful of windows and a landlord with views on what can be fitted. A few well-chosen blinds often solve the whole problem.

Light-industrial units with offices

Units with a warehouse or workshop and a small office block at the front often have large front glazing and a mix of older and newer window styles. Reception and front offices need to look right from outside.

Mixed old and new windows

Where a building has been extended or part-refurbished, windows of different ages and sizes sit side by side. Frames, reveal depths and fixings can all differ, so each window is worth surveying rather than assumed from its neighbour.

Choosing blinds

Which blinds suit which Borehamwood situation

Edit suites and screen rooms: blackout or dim-out

Screens and projected images lose contrast when light leaks round the edges. Genuine darkness is a fitting problem more than a fabric one, involving side channels or a deep recess fit and a closed head. A good dim-out is sometimes enough. The survey is where that gets decided honestly.

Offices with screens in daylight: solar screen rollers

For ordinary desks facing sun, a roller in a solar screen fabric cuts glare while keeping the view and daylight. The [screen glare guide](/screen-glare-office-blinds-london) covers how the fabric is chosen.

Where heat is the bigger complaint, the [office overheating guide](/office-overheating-blinds-london) sets out what a blind can and cannot do.

Wide front glazing in units: vertical blinds

Wide windows and glazed fronts can suit vertical blinds where flexible control of light and privacy matters more than a flat finish. The window proportions and the look wanted should decide, not habit.

Meeting and review rooms: privacy

Glass-fronted meeting rooms need to be open most of the time and closed for the meeting that matters. The [meeting-room privacy guide](/meeting-room-privacy-blinds-london) explains why quick, repeatable blinds usually beat applied film.

Landlord or assessor questions about fire performance

If a fire risk assessor or landlord has asked about window blinds, the [fire retardancy guide](/fire-retardant-office-blinds-london) explains what is being asked for and the free first step.

How the recommendation is arrived at

Every office is different, so the survey comes first.

Most office blind quotes are a price list run against a window schedule. A survey is a different exercise: it establishes what each room is for, what it actually suffers from and what would fix it, which fairly often costs less than the obvious answer. The project can then be quoted around the real specification rather than a generic per-window estimate.

Questions businesses ask

Office blinds in Borehamwood, answered properly.

Do you cover Borehamwood?

Yes. Borehamwood is part of the regular area. Very Well Hung is based in Edgware and Simone covers Borehamwood alongside Bushey, Radlett and Watford. For larger projects, enquiries from further afield across London and Hertfordshire are also welcome.

What do I need to organise for the survey?

Access to the windows and details of parking. Tell Simone the postcode and whether the unit has reception or a building manager. Business parks may have parking rules for visitors. It helps to know before the visit. If the windows are in several rooms, please make sure someone can open the doors.

Can you work around an office that is occupied?

Occupied offices are the normal case. Most office blind jobs happen in a working office. The practical details, such as which rooms are done on which day, are agreed when the quote is confirmed and depend on the premises. Raise anything specific at the survey.

Can you make an edit suite properly dark?

It depends on the room. The survey decides. Blackout fabric blocks light through its face. The leaks are at the edges: down the sides, over the head and under the bottom bar. Getting a room dark means dealing with those. Each adds cost. Simone will tell you whether the room needs full blackout or whether a good dim-out is enough.

Is it worth doing if I only have a few windows?

Yes. You do not need a whole building. A small suite or one problem room is a reasonable job to ask about. Send a photo of the worst window with the postcode and Simone will say what she would suggest. The price is confirmed after the free survey.

My landlord has asked about fire-retardant blinds. What should I do?

Start by checking what the existing blinds are labelled. There is no blanket law that office blinds must be fire retardant. A fire risk assessor may expect fabric tested to a recognised standard. Photograph any label on the existing blinds and read the fire retardancy guide on this site, which explains the options in order of cost.
