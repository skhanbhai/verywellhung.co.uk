The local picture

The premises we see in Edgware

Edgware is more mixed than a business park town. Alongside ordinary offices there are practices, nurseries and care homes in buildings that were not designed for the job. These are the types of building worth describing and the window problem each tends to bring.

Offices above shops on the high street

Upper floors above retail often have older windows of mixed size and awkward reveals. Street-facing glass needs privacy and glare control without losing daylight. No two windows in a run can be assumed to match.

GP, dental and clinic practices in converted houses

A house turned into a practice keeps its domestic windows. Treatment and consulting rooms need patient privacy from the street or the neighbours while still letting daylight in. Waiting rooms need cords kept out of reach of visiting children.

Nurseries and small schools in converted houses, church halls and extensions

Children’s rooms mean cords are the first thing to check. Converted houses add sash and casement windows of different sizes. Halls and extensions bring tall glazing that is hard to reach and often takes the afternoon sun. Sleep and quiet rooms may need dim-out.

Care homes in large converted or extended houses

Residents’ rooms often need dim-out for sleep and full privacy for personal care. Windows are a mix of the original house and later extensions. Operation has to be safe and simple for staff to manage across a lot of rooms.

Small business units and offices on the edges of town

Units with a front office and a workshop or store behind usually have a few large windows facing the car park or road. Reception needs to look right from outside while the back office needs glare control on screens.

Choosing blinds

Which blinds suit which Edgware situation

Nurseries and schools: cordless or tensioned blinds

Where children use the room, the safest blind is one with no loop at all. The cord safety guide sets out what the rules cover for older blinds and what to do about the ones you already have.

Care homes and surgeries: cords, fire evidence and cleaning together

A blind in a care home or practice is looked at for cord risk and fabric evidence and for how it is cleaned. The guide to blinds in care homes and surgeries covers what the guidance says on each. Which fabric a building needs is a question for its fire risk assessment.

Consulting rooms and street-facing offices: privacy blinds

Privacy is intermittent in some rooms and constant in others. A consulting room needs to close fully when it has to. A meeting room above a shop needs a blind that is quick to use. The reasoning is in the meeting-room privacy guide.

Offices with screens in daylight: solar screen rollers

A roller blind in a solar screen fabric cuts glare while keeping the view out. It suits the front office of a unit or a street-facing floor above a shop. The detail on fabric choice is in the screen glare guide.

Landlord or assessor questions about fire performance

Commercial premises may be asked to show that window blinds meet a recognised flammability standard. If that has come up, the fire retardancy guide explains what is being asked for and the free first step.

How the recommendation is arrived at

Every office is different, so the survey comes first.

Most office blind quotes are a price list run against a window schedule. A survey is a different exercise: it establishes what each room is for, what it actually suffers from and what would fix it, which fairly often costs less than the obvious answer. The project can then be quoted around the real specification rather than a generic per-window estimate.

What the survey looks at
  • How each room is used and who uses it
  • Whether any room needs privacy or dim-out rather than just glare control
  • Any loops of cord or chain and where they sit in relation to children or residents
  • Window sizes and types. Where they differ from room to room
  • What the frames and reveals will take for fixing
  • Whether the landlord or building manager has rules on what can be fitted

Yes. They are part of the regular area. Stanmore, Mill Hill and Hendon are all close to Simone’s base and part of the area she covers regularly. Send the postcode with a photo of the windows and she can confirm.

What do I need to organise for the survey?

Access to the windows and an idea of where to park. Tell Simone the postcode and whether the building has a manager or reception. High street premises can have restricted parking or loading. It helps to know before the visit. If the windows are in several rooms, please make sure someone can open the doors.

Can you work around an occupied building?

Occupied premises are the normal case. Most jobs happen in a working office or practice. Which rooms are done on which day is agreed when the quote is confirmed and depends on the premises. In a nursery or care home, say so at the start so the visit can be planned around the people there.

Is it worth doing if I only have a few windows?

Yes. You do not need a whole building. A single consulting room or one problem window is a reasonable job to ask about. Send a photo with the postcode and Simone will say what she would suggest. The price is confirmed after the free survey.

My landlord has asked about fire-retardant blinds. What should I do?

Start by checking what the existing blinds are labelled. There is no blanket law that office blinds must be fire retardant. A fire risk assessor may expect fabric tested to a recognised standard. Photograph any label on the existing blinds and read the fire retardancy guide on this site, which explains the options in order of cost.
