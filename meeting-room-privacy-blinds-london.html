Office blinds · Meeting-room privacy

The glass meeting room has no privacy when it matters.

Intermittent privacy, and why the permanent solutions keep disappointing.

The room needs to be open and visible for most of the week and properly closed for the hour a candidate is being interviewed or a client is looking at numbers on the wall. Here is what works on internal partitions, whether the room is called a meeting room, a conference room or the boardroom, why applied film so often disappoints, and what changes on a street-facing ground floor.

Quick answers

The short version, before the detail.

What blinds work on a glass meeting room?

A dim-out roller on a slimline cassette, in most cases. Workplace privacy is almost always intermittent: open and visible most of the week, closed for an hour. That points to something quick and repeatable to operate rather than something permanently opaque, and a roller on a shallow cassette is exactly that. Where the run is wide, a vertical blind often suits it better.

Should we use frosted film instead?

Only if the privacy is genuinely permanent. Film solves the one hour and compromises the other thirty-nine. A glass-walled room is usually glass-walled on purpose, to borrow light into the middle of a floorplate and to keep the space feeling open. Film gives that up permanently in order to solve a problem that is intermittent.

Can we have privacy and keep the light?

During the day, yes. A light-toned screen fabric gives daytime privacy while keeping the room bright, because it is brighter outside the glass than inside it. After dark that reverses and the room becomes the lit side, so it stops working exactly when the office is still occupied in winter. That is the single most common surprise on this subject.

Diagnosis

Work out which kind of privacy you need

Almost every disappointing privacy installation comes from answering a question nobody asked precisely. These six are the questions worth answering first.

It is needed for an hour or two a week

Interviews, appraisals, a client looking at numbers, a difficult conversation. If that is the requirement, the room does not need to be private, it needs to be able to become private in about four seconds and go back afterwards. Everything about the specification follows from that distinction.

It needs to work one way, or both

Stopping people outside seeing in is a different job from stopping people inside being distracted by the floor. A fabric that solves one may not solve the other, and a room where the occupants keep glancing at the corridor has a concentration problem rather than a confidentiality one.

It is an internal partition, not a window

Partitions bring their own constraints and they are physical rather than aesthetic: head details with nothing to fix into, a track already occupying the top channel, and door swings that decide where a cassette can sit. These usually determine the answer more than the fabric preference does.

The room is used after dark

This rules out the daytime-privacy trick entirely. A light screen fabric that works beautifully at two in the afternoon becomes transparent from outside once the room is the brighter side, which in a London winter is most of the second half of the working day.

It is a ground floor facing the street

A different problem with a different answer. Passers-by can see in whenever it is brighter inside than out, and there is a security and a reputational dimension as well as a comfort one. A dim-out roller solves it and takes the light; a light screen fabric keeps the room bright and reverses after dark.

It is really about being overheard

Worth separating out, because blinds do very little about sound. Fabric absorbs a small amount of reflected noise within a room and does close to nothing about transmission through a glass partition or under a door. If the actual complaint is that conversations carry, that is a partition, seal and door specification, and no window covering will fix it.

Background

Why the obvious answers disappoint

The requirement is intermittent and the solution usually is not

Almost every unsatisfactory glass-partition installation comes from solving an occasional need with a permanent measure. The room is then permanently less good at the thing it was built for, in exchange for being adequate at something it needs to do for an hour a week.

Glass partitions are glass on purpose

They exist to borrow daylight into the centre of a floorplate, to make a space feel larger than it is, and increasingly because a visible meeting room is a deliberate cultural choice. Obscuring them permanently undoes a design decision somebody paid for, which is why the complaint after a film installation is so often about the room feeling like a cupboard.

Daytime privacy reverses after dark

Any privacy that works by brightness difference works only while the difference runs the right way. Screen fabrics and mirrored films both do this. Between about November and February, in a room lit in the late afternoon, the effect inverts and the room becomes more visible than it was before anything was fitted.

A blind is not manifestation

Large glazed screens and doors in a workplace generally need manifestation, the markings that make the glass visible so nobody walks into it, with the guidance set out in Approved Document K.

A blind does not serve that purpose, because a blind that is usually open leaves the glass exactly as it was. It is worth confirming the markings are there on their own account rather than assuming a new blind has taken care of it.

What to do about it

The options, cheapest first

As with glare, the first answer on the list costs nothing, and it is right more often than anyone selling blinds would like.

1

Change which room the conversation happens in

If confidential conversations happen twice a month and there is a solid-walled room somewhere on the floor, booking that instead is free and immediately effective. Worth ruling out first. It stops being the answer as soon as the requirement is weekly, or the glass room is the only room with a screen in it.

2

Applied film, where the privacy is permanent

Where a room genuinely should never be seen into, a medical consulting room or an HR office, film is a sound and economical answer and there is no reason to fit a blind instead. The test is honest and simple: if the answer to "should this room be private on a normal Tuesday?" is yes, film is a reasonable choice.

3

A dim-out roller on a slimline cassette

The usual answer for an internal partition. Fast to operate, repeatable, and out of the way when it is not in use. The cassette keeps the rolled fabric tidy at the head, which matters more on an internal glass wall than on a window because the whole assembly is at eye level and on show from both sides.

4

Vertical blinds where the run is wide

On a long glazed run, verticals give graduated control rather than an all-or-nothing choice, and they are replaceable slat by slat, which matters when a floorplate has a lot of them and something eventually gets damaged. They are also the system most likely to conflict with a door swing, so where the door sits decides a great deal.

5

A light screen fabric for daytime privacy

Keeps the room bright and gives obscurity while it is brighter outside than in. Good in a daytime meeting room with an outside wall. Not the answer for a room used in the evening, and not the answer where the privacy requirement is absolute rather than a matter of discouraging casual glances.

6

Where the cassette can physically go

The specification that gets decided on site rather than on paper. Partition head details, existing tracks, sprinklers, ceiling grid and door swings routinely rule out the first choice, and finding that out at installation rather than at survey is what turns a straightforward job into a return visit.

Industry guidance on installation and on internal blind safety is published by the British Blind and Shutter Association, the national trade association for the blind and shutter industry.

Questions businesses ask

Meeting-room privacy, answered properly.

What blinds are best for glass meeting rooms?

A dim-out roller on a slimline cassette suits most internal partitions. Workplace privacy is almost always intermittent: the room needs to be open and visible for most of the week and closed for an hour, so what matters is that it is quick and repeatable to operate rather than permanently opaque.

On a wide run a vertical blind often works better, because it gives graduated control and can be replaced a slat at a time.

Is frosted film better than blinds for a meeting room?

Only where the privacy is genuinely permanent. Film solves the one hour a week and compromises the other thirty-nine, and a glass-walled room is usually glass-walled on purpose, to borrow daylight into the middle of a floorplate and keep the space feeling open.

For a consulting room or an HR office that should never be seen into, film is a sound and cheaper answer. For a meeting room that is normally open, it is the wrong tool.

Can we have privacy and still keep the daylight?

During the day, yes. A light-toned screen fabric obscures the room while it is brighter outside the glass than inside it, so the room stays bright and the view in is broken up.

The important caveat is that it reverses after dark: once the room is the lit side, it becomes more visible than it was. In a London winter that happens well before the end of the working day, so it depends entirely on when the room is used.

Do blinds help with meeting rooms being overheard?

Barely, and it is worth saying plainly. Fabric absorbs a little reflected sound inside the room and does almost nothing about transmission through a glass partition, around its edges or under the door.

If the real complaint is that conversations carry, the answer is in the partition, the seals and the door rather than in anything fitted to the glass. Anyone selling you a blind as an acoustic solution is overselling it.

What about privacy on a street-facing ground floor?

A different problem from an internal partition, and usually a more serious one. Passers-by can see in whenever it is brighter inside than out, which through a London winter is most of the working day. A dim-out roller solves it completely and takes the daylight with it.

A light screen fabric keeps the room bright and gives daytime obscurity, but reverses after dark, which matters if the room is used in the evening or if there is equipment on show.

Will a blind satisfy the requirement for markings on glass partitions?

No, and it is worth separating the two. Large glazed screens and doors generally need manifestation, the markings that make the glass visible so people do not walk into it, and the relevant guidance sits in Approved Document K.

A blind is not manifestation, because a blind that is normally open leaves the glass exactly as it was. This is not a compliance service Very Well Hung provides, and it is worth checking the markings on their own account rather than assuming a new blind has covered it.

How quickly can the blinds be closed when a meeting starts?

The operating method matters more than the fabric on a meeting room. A chain-operated roller on a two-metre partition takes a few seconds. A wide run of verticals takes longer and involves walking the length of it. If a room is booked back to back and needs to switch state between meetings, that difference is the specification.

Can the blinds match across several meeting rooms?

Within one order, yes, and that is the moment to do it. The same fabric from the same supplier shifts between dye lots, so rooms specified a year apart can be visibly different even with an identical fabric name on the paperwork.

Where several rooms front a client-facing corridor, ordering the run together is worth more than it costs. In back-of-house rooms it usually does not show and does not matter.
